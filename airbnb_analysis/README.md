# Midwest Airbnb Analysis

## Key Findings

1. Hosts with ten or more listings control 37.1 percent of Columbus's market, signaling a far more commercialized rental environment than the Twin Cities (17.2 percent).
2. Chicago's top 10 percent highest-earning listings generate 40.7 percent of the city's total estimated revenue, demonstrating strong income concentration at the high end of the market.
3. Twin Cities entire-home listings generate a median annual revenue of $19,619, giving prospective investors a realistic baseline for yearly gross income.
4. Twin Cities Superhosts charge $1.38 less per night than non-Superhosts ($266.93 versus $268.31), meaning new hosts cannot rely on the badge alone to command higher prices.
5. Performing an anti-join reveals 456 listings missing availability records entirely, with Chicago losing the most at 267 missing listings.
